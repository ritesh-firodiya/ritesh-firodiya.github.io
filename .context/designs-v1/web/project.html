The problem

Every Indian tax calculator answers one question — which regime is cheaper for you, one person, this year. That is the wrong unit. Tax in an Indian household moves across people: a parent's medical premium, a spouse's home-loan interest, a child's tuition. Filed one at a time, a family routinely leaves a deduction on the table that was always claimable.

What it does

  • Takes a Form 16 upload and parses it into a structured return.
  • Shows old and new regime side by side, with the delta stated in rupees, not in a chart.
  • Adds a household lens — spouse, parents, kids — and surfaces deductions that only exist across people.
  • A 15-question Quick Check for anyone who does not have a Form 16 to hand.
  • Prints the refund you can claim and the deductions still unused, as a document you can act on.

How it is built

One Next.js app on Cloud Run, roughly 189 Firebase Functions behind it, Firestore for state, and a Turborepo holding the lot. The decision that mattered was the contract.

Architecture diagram slot

Decisions worth defending

Zod as the only contract

Tax rules change every budget. A schema shared by the form, the function and the stored document means a rule change is one edit with the type system finding the rest. No hand-written DTOs on either side.

189 small functions, not one big service

Each deduction is its own function with its own tests. Wrong numbers are the only failure mode that matters here, and this shape makes a wrong number traceable to one file.

You see the verdict before you pay

A tax tool that shows a number and then asks for money before revealing it has already lost the user's trust. Which regime wins, and by how much, is free. The full workings and the household lens are the paid tiers — ₹249, ₹499 or ₹799 for an assessment year.

Where it stands

Live at chitragupt.ai and in use through the filing season. Next up is a multi-year view, so a household can see what a decision this year costs or saves the next.
