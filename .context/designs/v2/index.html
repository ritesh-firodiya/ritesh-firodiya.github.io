What is wrong with the site today

Six problems, all structural. A restyle alone fixes none of them.

  1. Problem 1 · the home page

    It makes you choose a lane before it shows you anything.

    "Three doors" — You are hiring / You need it built / You are curious — sits at band two, and the first product does not appear until band four. Every reader has to classify themselves correctly before the page gives them anything, and most guess wrong.

    Fix: lead with the work. It serves all four readers at once.

  2. Problem 2 · /products

    Thirteen rows of text and not one picture.

    The table was a deliberate reaction to the old marketing page, and it was right to kill the pitch. But six products have real screenshots sitting unused in public/media/, and nothing can be filtered.

    Fix: a visual grid with filters; keep the table as a density toggle.

  3. Problem 3 · /work

    The one long-form case study is orphaned.

    /work/chitragupt is linked from exactly one place: the bottom of the Chitragupt product page. There is no /work index, and the nav never mentions it. The strongest asset for recruiters and clients alike is effectively hidden.

    Fix: /work becomes a real page and a nav item.

  4. Problem 4 · the screens

    541 screens behind a nav item called "Screens".

    The design sets are the most unusual thing on this site and the hardest to find. The gallery is a flat list of links with no preview, no grouping by flow, and no reason given for why anyone should open it.

    Fix: previews, grouped by product and flow, with a sentence saying why it exists.

  5. Problem 5 · navigation

    Three nav links for eight destinations.

    Products · Screens · Résumé. Hire, work, support and legal are reachable only from the footer or from prose links inside pages.

    Fix: four honest destinations, Résumé always a button.

  6. Problem 6 · no dark mode

    One fixed light theme, on a site read mostly by engineers.

    Every token is already a CSS variable in a Tailwind v4 @theme block, so the cost of a second palette is one media query and a toggle — it was simply never done.

    Fix: both themes, whichever direction wins.

Why work-first serves all four

  • Recruiter — thirteen shipped things is the seniority argument. Résumé is a button in the header, so they never scroll for it.
  • Client — "can this person actually ship" is answered by pictures in the first screen, not by a services list.
  • App user — they arrived for one app. A filterable grid gets them there in one click; the old table did not.
  • Peer — 541 designed screens and a public honesty rule is the interesting part, and it is currently the best-hidden part.

Nothing here weakens the rule the site exists for. The model still sits above every install button, no claim is made on behalf of every app, and an unavailable platform still renders disabled with its reason.

The three directions

Live previews — click a title to open it full size. Each is the home page only.

A · Shelf →

light · Inter · showroom

A product studio's shelf. Cards with real device shots carry the page; type is one neutral sans doing all the work. Closest to how people expect to browse apps.

Strongest for app users, clients

Weakest for peers — says little about how you work

B · Ledger →

dark · mono · data-first

The site's actual premise made visible: every product is a checked row — model, ads, per-platform state, screen count, verified date. The honesty rule becomes the design rather than a paragraph about the design.

Strongest for peers, careful recruiters

Weakest for app users — dense, and dark portfolios are common

C · Editorial II →

warm paper · Fraunces · magazine

Keeps the identity you already have — warm paper, burnt sienna, Fraunces — and fixes only the structure. Same six fixes, familiar surface. The lowest-risk option and the one closest to today.

Strongest for clients, peers; keeps brand continuity

Weakest for you said you disliked the current UX — this looks most like it
