Process · idea → money

Eight stages, and every gate is a number.

This is how one person ships apps without a team. It is not advice — it is the actual working document, written down because a process that only exists in someone's head cannot be audited, handed over, or improved.

Most process writing stops at "it builds". That is the halfway point.

Stage The gate — answered with a number, not a feeling
1  IdeaOne sentence: who it is for, what it does, instead of what.
2  ResearchA keyword with real search volume exists. If nobody searches for it, nobody finds it.
3  ValidateIncumbents are weak — measured by reading their negative reviews, not by looking at their screenshots.
4  BuildDesigns approved, then schema approved. Two hard gates. The five stages →
5  LaunchListing complete, screenshots up, review prompt actually shipping.
6  MarketOne channel at a time, 30 days before judging it. Change one thing.
7  MoneyInstall → paid conversion is a known number, not a guess.
8  ImproveRanked from review themes and funnel drop-off — never from whichever idea is loudest that week.

Stages 1–4 are the part everyone writes about. Stages 5–8 are the part that decides whether any of it was worth doing, and they are where a solo developer actually loses.

Three rules that shape all eight.

Never fabricate a number

A failed scrape must not quietly read as "not ranked" — that invents a regression in the next report. It throws, or records an explicit null. Every total names what it excludes.

Claims are whitelisted before they can be said

Every claim any marketing surface may make is written down once, with the file that proves it. Anything not on the list cannot be published — including by me, which is the point.

Some steps refuse to be automated

The funnel report prints the four dashboards to read by hand rather than pretending to have numbers it does not have. A tool that fakes completeness is worse than one that admits a gap.
