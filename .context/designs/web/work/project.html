The problem

Every Indian tax calculator answers one question — which regime is cheaper for you, one person, this year. That is the wrong unit. Tax in an Indian household moves across people: a parent's medical premium, a spouse's home-loan interest, a child's tuition. Filed one at a time, a family routinely leaves a deduction on the table that was always claimable.

What it does

  • Parses a Form 16 upload into a structured return, rather than asking you to retype it.
  • Shows both regimes side by side with the delta stated in rupees, not drawn as a chart.
  • Adds a household lens across spouse, parents and kids, surfacing deductions that only exist between people.
  • Runs a 15-question Quick Check for anyone without a Form 16 to hand.
  • Prints what you can act on the refund claimable and the deductions still unused.

Decisions worth defending

  • Zod as the only contract — Tax rules change every budget. A schema shared by the form, the function and the stored document means a rule change is one edit with the type system finding the rest.
  • 189 small functions, not one service — Each deduction is its own function with its own tests, so a wrong number is traceable to one file.
  • The verdict is free — Which regime wins, and by how much, costs nothing. The paid tiers are the full workings and the household lens.
