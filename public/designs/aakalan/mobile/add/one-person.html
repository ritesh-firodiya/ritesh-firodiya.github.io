Splitting with one person, no group

Purpose: The most common split there is — one bill, one other person — without making anyone invent a group called "Me and Sana" first.

The With picker takes a person, not only a group. It used to offer "Just me" and then your groups, so splitting with one friend meant leaving a half-typed expense to go and invent a group. In the sheet a person and a group sit in the same list, sorted by how recently you used them — because to the question "who is this with", they are the same kind of answer. A second coffee with Sana is far more likely than a new group, so Sana is near the top.

Save moves to a sticky footer. It sat in the header, on the reasoning that the form should never need scrolling past to reach it. That reasoning is right and the footer serves it better: it is outside the scroll container, so scrolling cannot hide it either — and it is within reach of a thumb on a 6.7" phone, which the top-right corner is not. The receipt row already made this screen scroll.

Under the hood this is a two-person group, and the design has to be honest about the consequence. Not because a pair is a group, but because of who can read it: a personal expense lives in your own private corner, which nobody else can open. Anything the other person must be able to see has to live somewhere you both can reach. So the first time you do this with someone, the app says plainly that it becomes visible to them — a personal expense is private, this one is not, and that difference cannot be silent.

It is never shown as a group. No card called "Sana" in the Groups cut. It appears in the People cut, on the friend's ledger, on Spending and in the export — everywhere an expense belongs, and nowhere a group belongs.

Add a third person and it becomes a real group, and asks for a name at that moment. That upgrade is the reason this shape was chosen over inventing a separate kind of expense: nothing has to be migrated, because it was always a group.
