Purpose: All of onboarding is three screens — this, the OTP, and your name.

No welcome carousel. Nobody reads a value proposition before they have a reason to care. The pitch is one line above the phone field; the rest of the screen is the field.

Phone only. Google and Apple are gone. They were here so a returning user on a new device wouldn't wait for an SMS — a real saving of about fifteen seconds, once. What they cost is an account with no phone number, and the number is load-bearing in every other part of this app: contact matching finds people by it, invites are sent to it, a pair is merged on it, and the group document carries it so other members know whose row is whose. A Google account is a user who cannot be found, cannot be invited and cannot be added to a group by anyone who knows them. Requiring a number afterwards would make the button pointless; not requiring one makes the product broken.

The number is the identity, so it is the only way in. One field, one code, one screen.

No password anywhere in this product. For an app opened twice a month, a forgotten password is a churned user.

The +91 selector is a real selector. The app is India-first, not India-only, and a hardcoded country code strands anyone who travels.
