Balances · the whole splitter half

Purpose: One question — where do I stand — answered twice on one screen. The net, then the people it is made of.

This absorbed the Friends tab. The rule this screen already stated about a Groups tab — "a tab showing the same balance over the same list is the same screen twice" — applied just as well to Friends. It was the same hero, the same balances and the same Settle button, cut by person instead of by group. A cut is a segmented control, not a tab.

One list, not two. "Owes you" and "You owe" were two headed sections over rows that already say which is which, in the one place this product reserves a colour for: green is owed to you, rose is owed by you. Splitting them spent two headers and a gap re-stating what the amount was already saying — and it broke the sort, because the largest number on the screen could be stranded halfway down the second section. It is a single list now, biggest first, since the biggest number is the one that needs action. Each row keeps a two-word direction label above its figure: the same row shape groups/detail already uses for exactly this kind of mixed list.

The hero is compact, and it lost its Settle button. It used to be a 32px figure, two sub-cards and a full-width button — around 200px of screen to say one number, which pushed the people that number is about below the fold. The figure is 26px now, still four times the size of anything under it, so rank is unchanged; what went is packaging. The two sub-cards became the line beneath it. The button went because it opened a person picker listing the same people already visible: a tap to choose from a list, in order to reach a list. Every row carries its own settle button, and it arrives at the same screen with the person already chosen.

What is deliberately not here:

  • No pending-settlement banner. A claim someone made is a fact about that person, so it is an "Unconfirmed" flag on their row and the two buttons live on their ledger, one tap away. The banner reserved the top of the screen for a card that is absent most days, and it printed the same ₹2,500 twice — once in itself and once in the row directly beneath it. The flag on the row does not repeat the figure either, for the same reason: it is already in the right-hand column, six pixels away.
  • No header +. "Add expense" is the FAB; "New group" sits at the bottom of the Groups cut, where you are already looking at groups.
  • No separate contacts destination. Contacts continue the list, and search covers the whole address book.
  • No Account tab. The avatar in the header has always opened it, and a settings screen visited twice a year does not earn a permanent slot.

People is the default cut, not Groups. Every verb in this half of the app is per-person — settle, remind, confirm. A group is a container you open; a person is someone you owe. "What do I owe Sana" is also the question you get asked out loud, which Groups cannot answer without arithmetic.

Colour rule: green = you are owed, rose = you owe, and neither hue appears anywhere else — not on the hero, not on an avatar, not on a group cover. All square is neutral, not green. Green is spoken for; this state is the absence of a direction, not a positive one.

A group is a row, not a card. The Groups cut used to render media cards — a 46px full-bleed gradient band with an avatar stack on it, above the name and the amount, about 110px each — while the People cut next to it was 56px rows. One segmented control over one list produced two different kinds of object, which is a cut behaving like a change of medium. The band also carried nothing: the gradient was hashed from the name once the Type picker was cut, and the avatar stack on it said "5 people", which the subtitle underneath said in words. Groups are now the same .bal-row, marked with the overlapping avatar stack that the "With" picker already uses for a group — so a group still reads as a group, without a second component existing to say so. Six CSS classes went with the card.

And the label matches. Group cards said "You get back" where the person rows say "Owes you" — a third phrasing for a direction this screen already names twice, on a screen whose whole point is that the two directions look the same everywhere.

One list, ranked by readiness — no accordion. All-square people used to collapse behind a "2 people you're square with" line, which bought eight rows of height and cost the two things this list exists for: seeing who you split with, and tapping + to add the next expense. Zero is not a reason to hide a person. The one you are square with today is the one you split lunch with tomorrow, and the collapsed control made that person harder to reach than a name in your address book.

The order is open balances by size · square, on Aakalan · square, not on Aakalan yet · address book. Not alphabetical and not just by amount: it is how ready each row is to be used. Someone with an account you have already transacted with can be paid, settled and reminded right now; an address-book entry cannot be any of those until you add them. Rank by that and the top of the list is always the part that works.

Contacts continue the same list, immediately after the sync. One section, not two — "On Aakalan" and "Invite" restated a difference the button on each row already carries. They land directly under the people, with nothing collapsed in between, because the whole point of tapping Find friends from contacts is to see them.
