Receipt photos

Purpose: Answer "what was this ₹6,200?" three weeks later. A photo answers it and nothing else in the app does.

One photo, not an album. A bill is one piece of paper. Allowing five turns an expense row into a gallery and the question it answers into a browsing task.

The photo belongs to the expense, so it is visible to exactly the people the expense is. A group expense: every member. A personal one: nobody but you. The Storage rules mirror firestore.rules rather than approximating it — a readable image under an unreadable expense is a leak with extra steps.

Compressed on the device before it leaves. Long edge 1600px, JPEG. A bill is legible at that size, and a 12MP original is six megabytes of someone's data plan for no gain.

Nothing is read out of the image. No OCR, no amount guessed, no category inferred. The photo is evidence for a human, and an app that quietly fills in ₹6,200 from a blurry total is an app that will one day fill in ₹62,000.

Deleting the expense deletes the image. Product rule 5 keeps the record that something was removed — it does not require keeping a picture of it, and a photo nobody can reach from any screen is just a bill someone forgot they uploaded.

Pro, and visible to everyone. The camera button is on the free add screen with a Pro mark, not hidden. A feature nobody can see is a feature nobody buys — and hiding it would also make the row jump around when someone subscribes.

Uploads are not blocking. Saving an expense never waits for an image: the expense is written immediately and the photo follows, showing as pending on the detail screen. Offline is the normal case (rule 7), and a bill that cannot be saved in a basement restaurant is worse than one without a picture.
