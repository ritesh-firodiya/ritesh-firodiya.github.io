Person ledger

Purpose: The receipt you can show someone. Every line that produced the number at the top, across every group, in order.

The running balance column is the point. "You are owed ₹2,500" is a claim; a column that starts at zero and arrives at ₹2,500 is a proof. This is the screen that ends the argument the app exists to prevent, so it has to be legible to the other person, not only to you.

Groups are labels here, not sections. Splitting the ledger by group would break the running total, which is the one thing that makes it a ledger.

Remind is one tap and writes the message for you. The thing stopping people from chasing ₹2,500 is the awkwardness of composing the text, not the tap.

The name lives in the header, not in a second hero. The built screen spent its first 140px on a larger copy of the same avatar over the same name, pushing the balance — the thing you opened the screen for — most of the way down the fold.

At most two buttons on a line. It had four: Add expense, Remind, Settle up and Invite, each an icon plus a label, all flex-1 on a 390px phone. Remind shows only when they owe you, Settle up whenever anything is open, Invite only for somebody who is not on Aakalan — and Add expense takes its own full-width row.

Note the unconfirmed settlement at the top: it is in the list, greyed, and explicitly excluded from the running total. Product rule 6 — until Kabir's claim is confirmed it moves nothing, and pretending otherwise would make the balance lie.
