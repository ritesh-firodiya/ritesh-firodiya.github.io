Spending · the whole tracker half

Purpose: One month, summarised and then itemised. This is the expense-tracker payoff and the shared-expense audit trail, which turn out to be the same screen.

This absorbed Insights. Insights' own rationale said "a second screen to break them down would be a screen showing the same data again" — and that is exactly what Insights and Activity were to each other. One summed the month, the other listed it. Someone asking "where did my money go" had to visit both, and neither told them to. Now the summary is the header and the rows are the body.

Insights used to be a 20px icon while Settings had a whole tab. The tracker half of the pitch was the hardest thing in the app to find. It is now one of two tabs.

The filter governs the whole screen, not just the list. All / Just me / Groups re-cuts the total, the categories and the rows together. A filter that changes the list under a total it does not change is a filter that makes the screen lie — and "Just me" re-cut is the pure personal tracker, which is the thing Splitwise has no answer to.

A column of amounts, not a feed of sentences. "Arjun added an expense" cannot be scanned. Figures right-aligned and tabular can, so a whole month reads at a glance.

The right-hand number is always your share, never the bill total. On a ₹18,000 villa split five ways it reads ₹3,600 — because that is what it cost you, and it has to agree with the total at the top of the same screen.

Bars, not a donut. A donut needs a legend, and a legend means reading the value in one place and the label in another. Labelled bars put the category, the bar and the rupee figure on one line.

Product rule 5 is visible here. Deleted and edited expenses stay in the log as struck-through audit rows. An expense can be removed; the record that someone removed it cannot.

The only paywall that bites is history depth. This month and last are free; older months are Pro. Adding, splitting, settling and exporting are never gated — product rule 4, and the export button is in this screen's header to prove it.
