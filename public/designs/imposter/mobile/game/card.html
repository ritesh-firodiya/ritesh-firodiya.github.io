The card

v4 — the revealed card stays on the night flood. These frames used to flood to the role colour on open. Built, it fired a full-screen colour change mid-drag, and dragging back and forth across the commit threshold strobed the entire screen. A flood is a payoff for a discrete event; it is the wrong thing to bind to a continuous gesture. The role still reads instantly — the card itself carries it, a green word or a black IMPOSTER on white paper.

v5 — pass and reveal are one screen. v4 routed between /game/pass and /game/reveal once per player: ten navigations for a five-player deal. This is one card that lifts in place. Every app checked does it this way — imposter.app slides the card up, Imposter Who? flips it, Party Edition parks the whole table as chips along the bottom and swaps the word above them.

This card is the product. Everything else exists to get here and away again, which is why the word is hero size, nothing competes with it, and the stage is the role colour rather than a badge.

Swipe up, not tap. A thumb cannot do it by accident while the phone is changing hands. It is also the gesture the card's own shape suggests, so the affordance costs no copy.

The face-down state is the hand-off gate. It confirms identity, not intent: a half-watching player taps Continue by reflex, but they will not swipe up a card with someone else's name on it. Nothing here auto-advances — a countdown would show a card to whoever happens to be holding the phone.

Dots, not a roll-call. "2 of 5" and five dots. Never "Riya ✓ Arjun ✓" — who has already looked is information an imposter can use.

The secret panel is paper. White, black ink, physical. The civilian green is #86efac and unreadable as type; --civilian-fg is the readable weight of the same colour, which is what the fg tokens exist for.

One line under the word. v4 ran a paragraph, a "3 others see this too" chip and a pack chip alongside the one thing the screen exists to show. None of the three apps checked ships any of them.
