Settings

The promise is repeated at the top, not buried. Settings is where a suspicious user goes looking for the subscription they think they were tricked into. Finding the opposite, stated plainly, is worth more than any feature on this screen.

"You own Imposter Pro · Bought 4 Sep 2026" is a deliberate receipt. The recurring complaint in competitor reviews is a lifetime purchase that silently re-locks; a visible, dated ownership state is the cheapest possible answer to it.

Restore Purchases must stay reachable without scrolling past the fold on any device. App Store review requires it for a non-consumable, and it is the first thing a locked-out payer looks for.

There is no theme control, because there is no theme. Every screen is flooded — black outside a round, night inside one, role value at the payoff. A "Match device" row would be a switch with nothing behind it, and rule 9 says a control that cannot be operated is a sentence. Splash, Fakeit and Imposter Up each ship exactly one look too.

Sound and haptics are device-local and never synced — the person hosting is not the person who set the phone up.

No notifications section, because the app sends none. A party game has no business pushing anything.
