The product

A social-deduction party game for 3–20 people on one phone. Most players see a secret word; the Imposter sees only a hand-written one-word hint. Give a clue, argue, reveal.

The wedge is not a feature. Every app above 1M downloads in this category is disliked for exactly one thing — how it charges. So this one is free for three whole packs, $4.99 once for the rest, and carries no ad SDK at all. That sentence is the marketing, the onboarding and the paywall.

v5 · what playing the competitors changed

v4 was 22 files and 20 app routes for a game whose rules fit in four sentences. This pass played imposter.app end to end and pulled the store leaders — Imposter Who? (5M+ installs, 75K ratings) and Imposter Game · Party Edition (#2 Family chart, 90K ratings). Six things every one of them does, and v4 did none of:

There is no home screen. Launch lands on setup. Imposter Who? opens on four drill-down rows; imposter.app is one list and a Start button. v4 had a hero screen with a PLAY button, a "Same 5 again" card, a category grid and a Pro card — four different ways into a three-step wizard, each landing on a different step. Tapping a category set the pack and then asked for it again two screens later.

Pass and reveal are one screen. A card that lifts in place. v4 routed between /game/pass and /game/reveal once per player — ten navigations to deal five cards.

Nobody ships a vote screen. Imposter Who?'s own rules end "keep giving words and talking until someone thinks they've figured it out." imposter.app has one button: Reveal Imposter. The accusation belongs at the table, out loud — that is the fun part, and a tally UI moves it onto a phone nobody is looking at.

Nobody keeps score. Both end on Restart. Scoring in a party game is a rule five people have to agree to remember, and the apps that ship it are not the ones at 5M installs.

The card is the whole marketing. Three of the five App Store screenshots in every listing checked are the card and nothing else — a name and a word, no paragraph, no "3 others see this too" chip. v4's reveal had both, sitting next to the one thing the screen exists to show.

Setup rows are drill-downs. PLAYERS › PACKS › then controls. Three players or twenty, one pack or ten, the screen is the same five rows. That is the entire reason the unbounded things sit behind a chevron.

What was cut, and it is a lot. Mr. White, the vote tally, eliminations, repeat clue rounds, the Mr. White steal, scoring and the scoreboard. Thirteen wireframes deleted, three written. The game is now deal the cards → talk → reveal. What survived: hand-written hints on every word, the re-check, the hand-off gate, and the promise — the four things that came from reviews rather than from taste.

The token file was lying, and some of it never rendered. --amber-700 was referenced and never defined, so --shadow-press — the chunky 3D button edge v2 called a core decision — resolved to nothing on all 26 primary CTAs in the set. --violet-* held green in four slots and grey in three. --magenta-*, twenty --cat-* and eight --av-* variables had zero uses. --grad-imposter hardcoded rose and --grad-civilian teal — the two hues rule 1 forbids — inside a file whose own header forbids them. bg-surface0 in Settings and text-brand-500 in the player list were classes that do not exist.

What the market said

Pulled 2026-09-14. Imposter Who? 5M+ installs · Party Edition #2 in Family with 90K ratings · Imposter Game: Word Party Game 4.8 from 166 ratings after ten months. The category is large and not consolidated.

Pricing is the universal complaint. "$15.99 a week is insanity." "$81.99 a year." Accidental charges, refund fights, and lifetime purchases that silently re-lock. Answered by onboarding/promise promise · owns pro, store/paywall and settings.

"The round kept going after we found the imposter." Answered structurally in v5: there is no round left to keep going. One reveal ends it.

"We always forget what word we're on." Imposter Who?'s reviews. Answered by game/recheck, which no competitor ships — the one screen v5 kept purely because a review asked for it.

"Word: Will Smith, Imposter Hint: Slap — it feels like humans put thought into this." The best review in the whole set, and it is about hint quality. Every tile in setup/packs prints a hint count next to the word count.

Rules the design never breaks

1 · Light green is Civilian, black is Imposter. The palette is black, white and one light green — and no red at all — so the two roles are not hues borrowed from a wheel, they are values the app owns. There is no room for a pack plate or an avatar to take one, which is why both are a white plate carrying a lucide line icon: the drawing carries the meaning and the palette stays clean. There is no red even for destructive actions — that convention is older than this palette and it loses.

2 · Nothing may contradict the promise. No countdown, no strikethrough price, no "3 days free then billed weekly", no pre-selected plan, no delayed close button. Any one of them makes "No subscription. No ads. Ever." a lie.

3 · A locked pack is dimmed and labelled, never hidden. A pack nobody can see is a pack nobody buys — and hiding them makes seven tiles appear from nowhere the moment someone pays.

4 · Only one player may be looking at the phone during a reveal. The face-down card is a hard gate confirmed by name, never a timer, and the re-check needs a press-and-hold that hides on release.

5 · No screen makes the table do arithmetic. Setup prints "4 of you get the word. 1 is faking it."

6 · The clock never decides anything. Running out turns the ring white and stops. The table still chooses when to reveal.

7 · Nothing in the app needs a connection. No server, no account, no sync, no analytics. The one online call is the price lookup, and it must never block the start button.

8 · Every screen is flooded; only which colour changes. Brand outside a round — onboarding, setup, store, settings, states. Night inside a round while it is working — the card's face-down state, the talk screen, the re-check. Role colour at the payoff — the revealed card, and the answer. The green glow says "the app", unlit black says "a round is happening", colour says "here is the answer". There is no light theme and no theme switch: the flood is the theme, which is why Settings has no Appearance row.

9 · A control that cannot be operated is a sentence. The setup CTA says "Add 1 more player", not nothing. There is no Sounds toggle either, because there are no sounds.
